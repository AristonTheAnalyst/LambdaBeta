# Lambda — the human explainer

*How to talk about Lambda when someone asks. The rules live in `PRODUCT.md`; this is the story, in plain language. Use the shortest answer that fits, and keep the words on the avoid list out of it.*

## The shortest answers

**Five words**
A memory tool for lifting.

**One sentence**
Lambda gives your training history back to you — what you did last time, what you're chasing, and why you changed your plan.

**One paragraph**
Most people log their training in notes or a spreadsheet, then never look at it again because it's buried. Lambda is the same simple act of writing it down, with structure underneath, so any exercise's history is one tap away. It's descriptive, not analytical: no charts, no predictions, no AI. Five features, five kinds of memory. Did I mention that it was also free?

**The dinner-party version**
You know how you write your lifts in Notes and then can't find them two months later? Lambda is that, but structured. Tap the exercise, tap back, see every time you've done it. It also remembers your goals, your PRs, and why you changed your session plan. It doesn't tell you what to lift — it just gives you your own data back.

## What it is

Lambda is a memory prosthetic for resistance training — a tool that does the remembering for you.

We're forgetful. Notes and spreadsheets let you record training, but they can't give it back: the squat from two months ago is buried, unfindable without the right keyword, and therefore effectively forgotten. Lambda puts structure under the writing so retrieval is one tap.

The core question is always the exercise: *how was this exercise last time?* and *how has this exercise been?* Every view is a different time window on the same exercise history.

The value is assembly, not inference. "Analyse" means putting the pieces in one place, in a list you'd never build by hand — not charts, percentages, or predictions. Lambda reports what happened; you decide what it means. It is prescriptive about structure — three fields, one exercise at a time — and descriptive about output.

Five features, five kinds of memory:

- **Sessions** your reminder of what you've done.
- **Session plans** your reminder of what you've intended.
- **Goals** your reminder of what you're chasing.
- **PRs** your reminder of the best you've done.
- **Reviews** your reminder of why and what you've changed.

That's the whole app, it provides you confidence through evidence.

*I did that shit; I can do this slightly harder thing, probably.* 

The moment someone sees 30 PRs in a week they'd never have noticed, this is the product working.

## What it isn't

- **Not an analytics dashboard.** No charts, percentages, estimated maxes, trendlines, or inference. People don't read graphs; they read their own history.
- **Not an AI coach.** No generated plans, recommendations, predictions, or scores. It informs; you decide.
- **Not a social network.** No feed, public profiles, likes, or leaderboards. Training partners and coaches only, by friend code. Intimate on purpose.
- **Not a mode-switcher.** No coach mode or user mode for the application. Friend settings are configured on the same app.
- **Not a feature buffet.** The absence of features is the product.
- **Not a data-science project.** The original mistake. Trainees are good at inference if they see the data. The app is descriptive so the trainee can infer what's next.

## Who it's for

- **Beginners** who want to learn to read their own training before they pick up bad habits.
- **Experienced lifters** whose progress has stalled and who want to diagnose instead of program-hop.
- **Coaches and training partners** who want to see the data and talk about it.
- **Anyone who already logs training** and wants it back.

**Not for:** people who want a fixed program that tells them exactly what to lift, people who don't want to think about their training, and people who want an AI coach or a social feed. Those are different apps.

## How to answer the feature question

**"Will you add AI / auto-generated programs?"**
No. Lambda's job is to give you your own evidence, not to think for you. The moment it starts recommending what to lift, it becomes a different product — and a worse one for the people it's for.

**"Will you add charts, trends, estimated 1RMs?"**
No. People don't read graphs; they read their own history. Lambda assembles what you logged into a list you'd never build by hand. The conclusion is yours.

**"Will you add a feed, likes, leaderboards?"**
No. Social in Lambda is training partners and coaches: friend codes, visibility settings, no public surface. This is not Instagram 2.0.

**"Will you add a coach mode?"**
No. Coaching is a feature on the same app if you decide to use it for coaching.

**"Will you add programs?"**
Yes — committed, but not until the semantics are settled. A program is a set of session plans you do and review in a structured way. It's a structure you own, not a prescription the app hands you.

**"Will you add a calendar?"**
Maybe. It would be a view over your sessions and session plans, not a new thing to maintain. If it starts adding scheduling, recurrence, and reminders, it has to earn its place first.

**"Can you add [their feature]?"**
Maybe. The test is simple: which of the five features does it live inside, and does it restate what you logged rather than predict or judge? If it adds a mode, a feed, or inference, it isn't Lambda. If it passes, it goes on the list.

**"Why is it so minimal? Don't you want more features?"**
The absence is the product. Every feature is something the user has to learn and maintain. Five features, five kinds of memory. I'd rather add the sixth only when one of the five can't do the job.

**"Why not just use a spreadsheet?"**
A spreadsheet holds the same rows and gives you none of the retrieval. It can't show you how an exercise has been going in one tap, it doesn't know what a PR is, and it doesn't remember why you changed your session plan. Lambda is the structure and the memory; you still do the thinking.

**"How is this different from [popular app]?"**
Most training apps either deliver a program or sell you analytics. Lambda does neither. It's the memory layer: your history, your goals, your PRs, your reasons. You keep the thinking.

## Words that work

Remember, memory, history, find, one tap, your own data, what you did, what you're chasing, why you changed, session plan, five features, training partner, coach, simple, descriptive, assemble.

## Words to avoid

- **"Workout"** — ambiguous: it means both the plan and the session. Use **session plan** for the plan and **session** for what happened.
- **"Learns from you"** — implies the app adapts. It doesn't. *You* learn from your data.
- **"Smart"** — implies a model. PR detection is deterministic.
- **"AI"**, **"analytics"**, **"insights"**, **"optimise"**, **"platform"**, **"ecosystem"** — each sets an expectation Lambda doesn't meet, on purpose.
- **"SMART goals"** — goals are target sets.
- **"Estimated maxes"** — no inference.
- **"Full programs"** — programs are out of the UI.

## The one-sentence test

If you can't say what Lambda is without the words *AI*, *analytics*, or *platform*, you've drifted. The sentence is: **it remembers your training and gives it back.**

And the question for any feature: *does this give the user back something they already did, or does it tell them something they didn't know?* Lambda does the first.
